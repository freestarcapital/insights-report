01 · Overview

Purpose & Scope

This report consolidates findings from an asynchronous stakeholder discovery effort conducted as part of the Insights / Alerting initiative — an exploratory effort to surface monetization, operational, and reliability issues proactively, replacing or augmenting reactive dashboard-digging workflows with a high-signal, low-noise, actionable alerting experience.

The goal of this discovery phase was not to gather feature requests. It was to understand the human reasoning, heuristics, and operational workflows behind how issues are identified, triaged, and resolved today — before designing any tooling or automation.

Interviews were conducted asynchronously via Slack DM with cross-functional stakeholders across Customer Success and Yield Management. This document captures the questions asked, verbatim responses received, and a synthesized executive-level analysis of key themes.

Follow-Up Responses — May 12

Q: What alerts are you referring to? Any specific Slack channel or other platform?

I'm referring to the #daily-pubfig-intelligence, #pubfig-intelligence, and #weekly-rps-alerts Slack channels. I also check the FS Radar Looker dashboard multiple times a day — at least once when I start the day and once when I log off.

Q: What differentiates an alert that becomes actionable versus noise?

On weekdays: at least 50% drops, or a specific placement dropping >30% in the middle of the day. On weekends this is expected, so drops need to be very big (at least 70%). Most of the times when it drops a lot it's due to a site outage or the publisher removing tags — so it drops 90–99%.

Q: What signal combinations increase confidence something is genuinely wrong?

  • Requests + fill rate + impressions all dropping → site/tag/blocking investigation
  • CPM/RPM drops → more bidder/policy related

Q: When you investigate in PubDash/GAM, what are the first metrics you look at?

PubDash: Requests, Impressions, CPM across various dimensions. GAM: the same, plus Viewability and Fill Rate.

Q: Are there any alerts today that feel too noisy?

Yes. If the alert could tag the CSM directly, it would be great — that way I could silence the group until I'm mentioned.

Response — May 18

How he determines real issue vs. noise

Normally I pull up the hourly chart and see if it has recovered. From there I expand the lookback to see if it's within ranges for historical performance. Assuming all of that indicates a problem, I physically go to the site:

  • Do I see our ad tags? Often alerts are triggered by a publisher's departure or site going down for maintenance.
  • Is our ads.txt present? Often overlooked when pubs are pushing updates.

On seasonality vs. real signal

Seasonality would never cause a 50% drop in impressions except at the start of H1 and H2. Ad Requests is a metric I like — independent of fill, it helps inform whether something is broken.

Key metrics (in priority order)

MetricWhy It Matters
Ad RequestsIndependent of demand — best signal for platform health
ImpressionsClose second; susceptible to traffic/geo changes
CPMSimilar to impressions; nuanced by traffic quality
RevenueWhat publishers care most about; most nuanced
Pageviews & SessionsTraffic dictates performance regardless of tech health

Key observation on gradual degradation

Gradual decays in performance are less likely to be noticed.

Remediation steps

  1. CSM alerted of a dip and escalates to the publisher (if applicable)
  2. Review deployments to confirm if the dip aligns with any → rollback if so
  3. Site reviews: publishers sometimes remove tags, ads.txt, or change partners with no communication

05 · Executive Synthesis

Cross-Cutting Themes

01

Primary detection is Looker + manual habit, not systematic alerting

Across all respondents, the FS Radar Looker dashboard is the dominant daily monitoring tool for CSMs. Yield managers supplement it with BigQuery, GAM, and Partner UIs. Detection is largely reactive and manual — people check dashboards on a schedule rather than having issues proactively surfaced. Slack alert channels exist but are consumed inconsistently and perceived as noisy.

Implication

The first win is not building new detectors — it's making existing signals more actionable, better contextualized, and routed to the right person.

02

Operators use layered, multi-signal triage — not single-metric thresholds

The validated triage pattern across all respondents: start with revenue/impression anomaly → check traffic (GA) to separate traffic-driven from platform-driven drops → check ad requests → drill by dimension (domain → placement → demand partner) → physically visit the site → correlate with recent changes.

Implication

Alert policies should encode multi-signal logic. "Requests + fill + impressions all dropping" is materially different from any single metric in isolation. The platform should surface correlated signals together.

03

Context is the difference between noise and action

The most consistent pain point — whether explicit or implicit — was the lack of context attached to alerts. Martin wants alerts tagged to the responsible CSM. Destin notes gradual decays are missed. Kaloyan's triage involves four different tools before forming a hypothesis. Every respondent manually builds the narrative: what changed, when, how severe, who to notify.

Implication

The core value proposition is delivering alerts pre-enriched with context: estimated revenue impact, trend shape, correlated configuration changes, responsible account manager, and a root cause hypothesis.

04

Actionability thresholds are contextual, not universal

Martin: >50% drops weekdays, >70% weekends, >30% placement-specific mid-week. Destin: 50% impression drops are obvious worst-case; gradual decays go undetected. Kaloyan: uses 60–90 day trend baselines. Lindsay: looks at shape and cross-dimension correlation — no explicit numerical thresholds.

Implication

A universal threshold produces alert fatigue or missed issues. The system needs per-publisher baselines, day-of-week awareness, dimension-aware sensitivity, and trend shape classification.

05

The escalation graph is well-understood but not encoded in tooling

TriggerEscalation Path
Publisher/tag issueCSM → Publisher
Config change suspectedReview deployment history → Consider rollback
Demand partner issueDemand team → Partner conversation
Technical/site issuePSM → PSE → Engineering

Implication

V1 should encode the escalation graph — surface the right person alongside the right context in every alert. No alert today knows who the account manager is or what the next action should be.

06

Gradual degradation is a systematic blind spot

Both Destin and Kaloyan independently noted that gradual performance decays are less likely to be caught. Current alerting is optimized for sudden, large drops. The TripleLift example (multi-week decline since early April with no bounce-back, ticket #32758) is a real-world instance of a meaningful revenue issue that required sustained manual attention to surface.

Implication

Trend-shape detection (slope analysis, recovery rate tracking) and anomaly scoring against 60–90 day baselines should be first-class features. This is where AI-assisted analysis has the most defensible advantage over simple threshold rules.

06 · Validation

Notable Proof Point: Live AI Triage Validation

During the Martin Olcese conversation on May 12, an AI-generated alert triage analysis was shared in real-time for a live issue: daytondailynews.com (Cox First Media, site ID 6894), which had fired a >50% alert and was actively being investigated.

AI Triage Analysis — daytondailynews.com

May 12, 2026 · Live issue

What the analysis produced:

  • Identified the fill rate cliff onset as May 6 — the exact date of a batch Spanner configuration update across three Cox First Media sites
  • Estimated ~$729 in revenue loss
  • Flagged correlated sites (journal-news.com, springfieldnewssun.com) below the alert threshold but showing the same pattern
  • Produced a structured follow-up action plan with responsible contact and investigation priorities

Martin reviewed the output and responded with operational context the AI tool couldn't have known:

"This pub is in their own GAM. We cleaned up a lot of protection rules and removed the Pricing Rules they had, so we are investigating why it dropped (it coincides as well with a Direct Campaign increase). They are not using FS Floors — this is one of the theories as to why Prebid dropped (AdX won a lot more)."

He offered a concrete product suggestion:

"If the code can identify if it's in the Pub GAM, then maybe a sanity checklist can be suggested including checking pricing rules, protection rules, direct campaign inflow, and policy violations."

"That daily summary is amazing and accurate."

— Martin Olcese, Sr. Customer Success Manager · May 12, 2026

Why This Matters

This exchange represents real-world, in-context validation by an experienced operator on a live revenue issue — not a prototype demo. The fact that Martin immediately added context the tool couldn't see also illustrates exactly where human-AI collaboration has the most leverage: the tool surfaces the signal and the pattern; the human brings the operational and relationship context.
